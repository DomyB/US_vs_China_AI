## Alignment

Chile's index leans toward China, 41.7 against 11.2 in 2024 [index_value:influence:CN:2024] [index_value:influence:US:2024], and its trade is the largest in the region: US$68.4 bn of mineral exports in 2025, 48% to China and 14% to the United States, the highest US share of any country with a China lean [concentration:share_cn_x:2025] [concentration:share_us_x:2025]. Copper is US$58.6 bn of it, US$29.8 bn to China and US$8.7 bn to the United States [flow_trade:CHL:copper:2025]. The executives' coded statements are measured toward both powers (+0.20 toward the United States over ten, 0.00 toward China over three) [statement:stance_us:CHL:2024-2026] [statement:stance_cn:CHL:2024-2026]. Lithium takes 51% of the mineral mentions and 3.2% of the export value; copper is 86% of the value and a third of the mentions [insights:talk_vs_trade_minerals:CHL]. My reading: Chile is the country where the United States could matter commercially, because it already buys a seventh of the copper, and the one where the political conversation is least about that.

## Trajectory

The Chinese share of exports rose from 19% in 2008 to 44% in 2016 and 54% in 2020, then eased to 48% in 2025 [concentration:share_cn_x:2020] [concentration:share_cn_x:2025]. Nine sudden trade shifts are flagged, the most in the region [anomaly_flag:sudden_trade_shift]. Chinese commitments were US$4.9 bn over 2015–2021, US$3.0 bn of it in 2018, the year of the Tianqi stake in SQM; no US commitment is recorded [flow_finance:CN:CHL:2018] [flow_finance:US:CHL:2015-2021]. The window after the 2023 national lithium strategy shows the share to the United States rising 3.6 points (placebo 0.09), the only policy window in the region with a US-side movement in that direction [event_effect:chl_national_lithium_strategy:US:window:CHL]. ## Risks and likely next moves

The models put China's share at 54% in 2030 at baseline, 64% under the China pull and 44% under US reshoring; the US share reaches 24% under reshoring, the highest of any country [forecast:export_share:CN:baseline:2030:CHL] [forecast:export_share:US:us_reshoring:2030:CHL]. Parity would take US$11.6 bn of yearly copper switching, 1.2 times what the United States already buys, and copper alone could cover it [insights:parity_gap:CHL]. The Section 232 copper proclamation of 2025 exempts ores, concentrates and cathodes, so in my reading it changes little for Chile directly [event:us_section232_copper]; the Codelco–SQM lithium company and the new lithium contracts decide where the second mineral goes. The risk is price: a copper price swing revalues the Chinese and American bills in the same proportion and moves no share at all [price:copper].
