## Alignment

Suriname has the deepest China lean in the region, 45.7 against 8.3 in 2024, on four components each [index_value:influence:CN:2024] [index_value:influence:US:2024]. The lean does not come from trade: the US$1.6 bn of mineral exports in 2025 are gold with no reported flow to either power [concentration:share_cn_x:2025] [flow_trade:SUR:gold:2025]. It comes from money, debt and votes: US$747 m of Chinese commitments over 2015–2021, nine of them flagged as large relative to GDP and seven as rescue lending, debt owed to China at 11% of GDP in 2024, and a single US item of US$10 k in 2016 [flow_finance:CN:SUR:2015-2021] [anomaly_flag:large_commitment] [anomaly_flag:rescue_lending] [index_component:debt_stock:CN:2024] [flow_finance:US:SUR:2015-2021]. Three executive statements are coded (+0.33 toward China over three, none on the United States); no legislative record or headline is coded [statement:stance_cn:SUR:2024-2025] [coverage:SUR]. My reading: Suriname's dependence is a debt relationship that the mineral trade does not show, and the index records it correctly as influence of a different kind.

## Trajectory

The Chinese index fell 13.1 points over the five years to 2024, the largest fall in the region, as the loan years of 2016 and 2019 moved out of the three-year finance window [index_value:influence:CN:2024] [flow_finance:CN:SUR:2016]. The US index rose 6.3 points from a very low base [index_value:influence:US:2024]. No event window stands out: the US share moves by three points on a base of a few million dollars [event_effect:SUR].
