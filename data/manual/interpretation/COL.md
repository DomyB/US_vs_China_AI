Colombia is the exception to the regional pattern. Documented commitments are also balanced: US$1.1 bn from Chinese institutions and US$1.1 bn from the DFC and EXIM over 2015–2021, then US$261 m from the US institutions in 2022–2024 [flow_finance:CN:COL:2015-2021] [flow_finance:US:COL:2015-2021] [flow_finance:US:COL:2022-2024]. The index still leans toward China, 31.8 against 17.2 in 2024, because the UN-voting component pulls that way (78% agreement with China against 39% with the United States) [index_value:influence:CN:2024] [index_value:influence:US:2024] [index_component:diplomatic_alignment:CN:2024]. My reading: Colombia's mineral ties run to the United States and its index runs to China, which says the index measures recorded ties of several kinds, not trade alone.

## Trajectory

The Chinese share of exports has never been large: 12% in 2008, 5% in 2016, 10% in 2020, 8% in 2025 [concentration:share_cn_x:2008] [concentration:share_cn_x:2025]. The nickel and copper that do go to China (US$274 m and US$174 m in 2025) are the exceptions inside a gold economy [flow_trade:COL:nickel:2025]. Seven sudden trade shifts are flagged [anomaly_flag:sudden_trade_shift]. The window after the 2017 US critical-minerals order shows the share to the United States falling 26 points and the share to China rising 8, a gold-market movement that no US policy explains [event_effect:us_eo13817_critical_minerals:US:window:COL] [event_effect:us_eo13817_critical_minerals:CN:window:COL]. The twelve coded statements are too few for a pooled stance; the two by US officials are both negative on China [statement:count:COL] [statement:count:bloc=United States:COL]. The legislature's coded records are sparse and positive toward both powers in 2020 [document:stance_cn:COL:2020].
